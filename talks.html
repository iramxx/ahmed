When Math Learned to Think: Transformers & Large Language Models

Attention Mechanisms / QKV Projections / Positional Encodings / Softmax Geometry / Latent Spaces

⚠️

"How did mathematical equations start demonstrating synthetic reasoning?"

This lecture unpacks the mathematical engine behind modern Transformers and Large Language Models. Rather than treating deep neural networks as black boxes, we dissect the rigorous linear algebra, geometry, and probability that enable models like ChatGPT to reason.

Key topics covered include:

  • Semantic Vector Spaces: Tokenization, word embeddings, and contextualized representation spaces.
  • Self-Attention Mechanism: Query, Key, and Value ($Q, K, V$) projections, scaled dot-product scoring, and geometric interpretability.
  • Multi-Head Attention: Orthogonal subspace projections enabling concurrent linguistic and relational reasoning.
  • Positional Encodings: Sinusoidal embeddings and Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) overcoming permutation invariance.
  • Softmax Normalization & Residual Streams: Gradient highways, layer normalization dynamics, and information propagation.

The Math Behind AI, ML, and Neural Networks

Probability / Optimization / Linear Algebra

"Some people think AI is magic. I spent 13 hours researching and 9 hours designing to prove it's not."

March 2026 Collab with Adham (President of IB&CM Society)

The Numbers Behind Trade: When Equations Meet Wall Street

Arbitrage / Options Pricing / Black-Scholes PDE / Itô's Lemma / Quant Finance

A collaborative talk bridging the gap between mathematical theory and financial markets. We explored how continuous mathematical models are utilized by quantitative analysts to price derivatives and manage risk.

Key topics included the fundamentals of arbitrage, risk-neutral probability, binomial models for pricing options, and how continuous stock paths through Brownian motion and Itô's Lemma lead to the famous Black-Scholes equation.
